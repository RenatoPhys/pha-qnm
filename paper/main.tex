\abstract{%
We compute all three homogeneous quasinormal sectors of the
maximum-likelihood realization of a lattice-calibrated
Einstein--Maxwell--dilaton model. Reference-compatible continuation and an
independent cusp condition locate the critical endpoint at
approximately 103.755 MeV and 593.323 MeV and resolve the coexistence and
spinodal lines. At this background, source-factored Chebyshev collocation and
complex shooting independently validate the leading quintuplet and singlet
poles and two triplet poles. Their least-damped dimensionless rates are
0.8669, 0.1032, and 0.2561, respectively. Along zero baryon chemical potential
and a fixed chemical-potential-to-temperature ratio of two, the triplet rate
decreases nonmonotonically to 0.0037 while the pole remains oscillatory.
Pointwise shooting and eigenfunction overlaps above 0.9985 confirm both
trajectories. Repeating the calculation with the earlier 2018 EMD parameters
reproduces nine published relaxation times within 0.012 fm/c. The analysis is
restricted to the maximum-likelihood realization and to homogeneous response;
it neither represents posterior uncertainty nor infers diffusion, sound, or
spinodal growth.}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Real-time response at nonzero baryon density is a central open problem in the
theory of the quark--gluon plasma. Lattice QCD tightly constrains equilibrium
physics around vanishing baryon density, but direct real-time calculations and
the finite-density regime remain difficult. Bottom-up Einstein--Maxwell--dilaton
(EMD) models provide a controlled strongly coupled laboratory in which the
equilibrium equation of state and retarded response arise from a common black
brane geometry.

The critical endpoint is a dynamical as well as an equilibrium problem.
Conservation laws and mode coupling determine critical slowing down, and the
QCD critical point is expected to belong to Model H
\cite{Hohenberg:1977ym,Son:2004iv}.  Classical large-$N_c$ holography need not
realize the same transport enhancement, so the dynamic exponent and the
reorganization of poles must be measured within the model rather than assumed
from the static equation of state \cite{DeWolfe:2011ts,Roth:2024hpn}.

The Bayesian analysis of ref.~\cite{Hippert:2023bel} calibrated two EMD
parametrizations to lattice data and inferred a critical endpoint for nearly
all posterior samples.  Its public posterior preserves the complete model
provenance \cite{HippertZenodo}, while ref.~\cite{Khan:2026uq} evaluates
thermodynamics and transport across the same phase diagram.  This work uses
only the maximum-likelihood polynomial--hyperbolic realization.  Accordingly,
the results below are model predictions at the MAP point, not posterior
credible intervals.

Several complementary holographic results delimit the open problem.
Homogeneous nonhydrodynamic modes have been followed near critical points in
both top-down and QCD-calibrated EMD models
\cite{Finazzo:2016psx,Rougemont:2018taa}.  Spinodal sound instabilities and their
preferred wavelengths are known in Einstein--scalar models
\cite{Janik:2015waa,Janik:2016btb,Caddeo:2024qgm}, and charged-fluid studies
show that pole collisions control the convergence of hydrodynamics
\cite{Abbasi:2020ykq,Jansen:2020hfd}. Recent improved-holographic-QCD, V-QCD,
and Einstein--dilaton
calculations further illustrate the variety of finite-density instabilities
and channel spectra \cite{Alho:2020gwl,CruzRojas:2024lwo,Villarreal:2025qtm}. What remains
missing for the PHA realization is a validated treatment of the coupled
finite-momentum sectors across thermodynamic branches.

Relative to the homogeneous EMD analysis of ref.~\cite{Rougemont:2018taa},
the present calculation uses the public PHA maximum-likelihood realization,
reconstructs its full equilibrium phase structure, validates every reported
pole with a second radial method, and follows all three $SO(3)$ sectors on
thermodynamically defined trajectories. It also reproduces nine relaxation
times quoted in ref.~\cite{Rougemont:2018taa} after substituting that model's
published functions and parameters into the present pipeline. Its novelty is
therefore a controlled MAP-model prediction for homogeneous response, not a
claim about the dynamic universality class of the endpoint.

Here we give a closed homogeneous-response analysis without assigning
coupled-channel physics to decoupled equations. We integrate the exact MAP
background in a conserved-charge formulation and compute the quintuplet,
triplet, and singlet $SO(3)$ sectors. Source factoring, separate resolution and
UV-domain studies, left--right conditioning, eigenfunction overlaps, and a
complex shooting calculation validate four poles at the independently located
critical background. Three additional eigenvalues of the original discrete
pencil fail the shooting test and are discarded.

We separately reproduce the public 2023 constant-$\phi_0$ crossing algorithm,
locate the cusp from the singular thermodynamic map, and construct the
equal-pressure coexistence line. The two local critical determinations agree,
but not with the critical coordinates stored in the posterior HDF5 metadata;
we report this provenance discrepancy rather than retuning the model. We then
continue the validated leading poles along $\mu_B=0$ and $\mu_B/T=2$ using
shooting and overlap tracking. Coupled finite-momentum dynamics is deliberately
outside the claims of this homogeneous paper.

\section{PHA EMD model and phase structure}
\label{sec:model}

We use natural units, mostly-plus signature, and set the asymptotic AdS radius
to one. The action is
\begin{equation}
 S=\frac{1}{2\kappa_5^2}\int \dd^5x\sqrt{-g}\left[
 R-\frac12(\partial\phi)^2-V(\phi)-\frac14 f(\phi)F_{MN}F^{MN}\right].
 \label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
The polynomial--hyperbolic Ansatz is
\begin{align}
 V(\phi)&=-12\cosh(\gamma\phi)+b_2\phi^2+b_4\phi^4+b_6\phi^6,\nonumber\\
 f(\phi)&=\frac{\operatorname{sech}g(\phi)
 +d_1\operatorname{sech}(d_2\phi)}{1+d_1},
 &g(\phi)&=c_1\phi+c_2\phi^2+c_3\phi^3.
 \label{eq:pha}
\end{align}
All derivatives used by the solver are analytic. In particular,
\begin{align}
 V_\phi={}&-12\gamma\sinh(\gamma\phi)+2b_2\phi+4b_4\phi^3+6b_6\phi^5,\\
 V_{\phi\phi}={}&-12\gamma^2\cosh(\gamma\phi)+2b_2+12b_4\phi^2+30b_6\phi^4,\\
 f_{\phi\phi}=\frac{1}{1+d_1}\bigg\{
 &\operatorname{sech}g\left[\tanh^2g-\operatorname{sech}^2g\right](g')^2
 -\operatorname{sech}g\tanh g\,g''\nonumber\\
 &+d_1d_2^2\operatorname{sech}(d_2\phi)
 \left[\tanh^2(d_2\phi)-\operatorname{sech}^2(d_2\phi)\right]\bigg\}.
 \label{eq:fpp}
\end{align}

Figure~\ref{fig:kernels} displays the resulting functions. The narrow structure
in $f(\phi)$ near the boundary is controlled by the large value of $d_2$ and is
numerically resolved rather than smoothed or dropped.

\subsection{Black-brane backgrounds}

In domain-wall gauge,
\begin{equation}
 \dd s^2=e^{2A(r)}[-h(r)\dd t^2+\dd\bm{x}^2]+\frac{\dd r^2}{h(r)},
 \qquad A_M\dd x^M=\Phi(r)\dd t.
 \label{eq:background}
\end{equation}
The equations integrated by the reference path are
\begin{align}
 \phi''+\left(4A'+\frac{h'}h\right)\phi'
 -\frac1h\left(V_\phi-\frac12e^{-2A}f_\phi\Phi'^2\right)&=0,\\
 \Phi''+\left(2A'+\frac{f_\phi}{f}\phi'\right)\Phi'&=0,\\
 A''+\frac16\phi'^2&=0,\\
 h''+4A'h'-e^{-2A}f\Phi'^2&=0,
 \label{eq:background-eoms}
\end{align}
with constraint and conserved charge
\begin{align}
 \mathcal C&=h(24A'^2-\phi'^2)+6A'h'+2V+e^{-2A}f\Phi'^2=0,\nonumber\\
 \mathcal Q&=e^{2A}f\Phi'=\text{constant}.
 \label{eq:diagnostics}
\end{align}
The numerical gauge is $r_H=A_H=h_H=\Phi_H=0$, $h'_H=1$, with independent
data $(\phi_0,\Phi_1)$. Regularity requires
$|\Phi_1|<[-2V(\phi_0)/f(\phi_0)]^{1/2}$.

The UV coefficients define
\begin{align}
 T&=\frac{\Lambda}{4\pi\phi_A^{1/\nu}\sqrt{h_0^{\rm far}}},&
 \mub&=\frac{\Phi_0^{\rm far}\Lambda}{\phi_A^{1/\nu}\sqrt{h_0^{\rm far}}},\nonumber\\
 s&=\frac{2\pi\Lambda^3}{\kappa_5^2\phi_A^{3/\nu}},&
 \rhob&=-\frac{\Phi_2^{\rm far}\Lambda^3}
 {\kappa_5^2\phi_A^{3/\nu}\sqrt{h_0^{\rm far}}}.
 \label{eq:thermodynamics}
\end{align}
Continuation is carried out in $(\phi_0,\Phi_1)$, not by isolated inversion in
$(T,\mub)$. Folds are detected from
$\det[\partial(T,\mub)/\partial(\phi_0,\Phi_1)]=0$.  Along a line of fixed
chemical potential, pressure differences follow from $\dd P=s\,\dd T$; the
coexistence temperature is where the integrated pressure difference between
the two stable branches vanishes.

Figure~\ref{fig:phase-diagram} shows the resulting first-order region.  A
$141\times121$ horizon-data surface supplies 65 constant-$\mu_B$ continuations
over $594\leq\mu_B/{\rm MeV}\leq850$.  Every line contains two folds and an
equal-pressure crossing.  The two spinodals and coexistence line meet at the
independently located cusp.  Repeating the construction on grids coarsened by
factors two and four changes the spinodal temperatures by at most $0.018$ MeV
and the coexistence temperature by at most $0.0085$ MeV.

\section{Linear perturbations and QNM sectors}
\label{sec:perturbations}

We transform to ingoing Eddington--Finkelstein coordinates,
\begin{equation}
 \dd s^2=-e^{2A}h\,\dd v^2+2e^A\dd v\dd r+e^{2A}\dd\bm{x}^2,
 \qquad A=\Phi(r)\dd v,
\end{equation}
and use $e^{-\ii\omega v+\ii kz}$. Infalling behavior becomes future-horizon
regularity. The primitive-field formulation is chosen so the discretized
system is at most linear in frequency,
\begin{equation}
 [M_0(k)+\omega M_1(k)]\bm v=0.
 \label{eq:pencil}
\end{equation}
This source-free, gauge-invariant prescription follows the standard holographic
QNM construction of ref.~\cite{Kovtun:2005ev}; coupled-field observables are
checked using the operator-mixing framework of ref.~\cite{Kaminski:2009dh}.

Throughout, $\omega$ and $k$ denote physical frequency and momentum,
$\w=\omega/(2\pi T)$ and $\q=k/(2\pi T)$ their dimensionless counterparts,
$\mub$ the baryon chemical potential, and $\rhob$ the baryon density. The
Fourier convention places stable poles in the lower half of the complex
$\omega$ plane. Horizon data are denoted by $(\phi_0,\Phi_1)$; no symbol in the
spectral tables is reused for a thermodynamic branch label.

At $k=0$, $SO(3)$ organizes perturbations into quintuplet, triplet, and singlet
sectors. At finite $k$ along $z$, they reorganize into $SO(2)$ helicities two,
one, and zero. Radial gauge is used only together with residual-gauge and
constraint diagnostics; source conditions are imposed on gauge-invariant
combinations.

The tensor/helicity-two equation is
\begin{equation}
 Z_2''+\left(4A'+\frac{h'}h-\frac{2\ii\omega e^{-A}}h\right)Z_2'
 +\left(-\frac{e^{-2A}k^2}{h}-\frac{3\ii\omega e^{-A}A'}h\right)Z_2=0.
 \label{eq:tensor}
\end{equation}
At $k=0$, the homogeneous vector master field obeys
\begin{multline}
 a''+\left(2A'+\frac{h'}h+\frac{f_\phi}{f}\phi'
 -\frac{2\ii\omega e^{-A}}h\right)a'\\
 +\left[-\frac{e^{-2A}f\Phi'^2}{h}
 -\frac{\ii\omega e^{-A}}h\left(A'+\frac{f_\phi}{f}\phi'\right)\right]a=0.
 \label{eq:vector}
\end{multline}
The source-free powers are $Z_2\sim u^4$ and $a\sim u^2$ for $u=e^{-A}$.
For the singlet, define
\begin{equation}
 {\cal S}=\varphi-\frac{\phi'}{2A'}\frac{H_{xx}+H_{yy}+H_{zz}}{3},
 \qquad Y=\frac{\phi'}{A'},\qquad \psi=\frac{{\cal S}}{Y}.
 \label{eq:singlet-invariant}
\end{equation}
The homogeneous master equation is
\begin{multline}
 \psi''+\left(4A'+\frac{h'}h+2\frac{Y'}Y
 -\frac{2\ii\omega e^{-A}}h\right)\psi'\\
 +\left[-\frac{h'}h\frac{Y'}Y
 +\frac{e^{-2A}\Phi'^2}{h\phi'}(3A'f_\phi-f\phi')
 -\frac{\ii\omega e^{-A}}h\left(3A'+2\frac{Y'}Y\right)\right]\psi=0.
 \label{eq:singlet}
\end{multline}
An independent first-order variation of the complete EMD system and the field
redefinition in eq.~\eqref{eq:singlet-invariant} reproduce
eq.~\eqref{eq:singlet}.  The same symbolic route generates the coupled
finite-$k$ kernels, but their spectra remain behind the numerical gates in
appendix~\ref{app:gates}.

\section{Numerical method and validation}
\label{sec:numerics}

Two numerical paths are retained. The compiled implementation supplies
analytic model kernels, a fourth-order horizon recurrence, background and
Chebyshev unit tests, and an independent assembly of the decoupled operators.
The reported scans use a separately implemented analysis path. The two routes
share the physical conventions but not the background integrator. Software
versions and regeneration commands are collected in appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\subsection{Background integration and ultraviolet fit}

Rather than evolving both $\Phi$ and $\Phi'$, the Python solver fixes
$\mathcal Q=f(\phi_0)\Phi_1$ at the horizon and reconstructs
\begin{equation}
 \Phi'(r)=\frac{\mathcal Q e^{-2A(r)}}{f[\phi(r)]}.
 \label{eq:reduced-charge}
\end{equation}
The remaining seven first-order equations are integrated from
$\epsilon_H=10^{-7}$ to $r_{\rm max}=12$ with the eighth-order DOP853 method,
relative tolerance $3\times10^{-12}$, absolute tolerance $3\times10^{-14}$,
and maximum step $0.025$. The ultraviolet coefficients are extracted on a
fixed 2400-point sampling grid, so an adaptive solver's point density cannot
bias the fit. The window $10^{-10}<\phi<10^{-4}$ gives the linear asymptote
$A=A_{-1}r+A_0$, while $\Phi_2=-\mathcal Q/(2A_{-1})$ follows directly from
Gauss' law.

Across the 90-point grid the normalized Einstein-constraint residual is at
most $3.50\times10^{-9}$ and the reconstructed Gauss-law drift is below
$4.45\times10^{-16}$. The relative spread of $\phi_A$ within the UV window is
between $1.25\times10^{-6}$ and $1.78\times10^{-5}$. At the background used for
the spectra these diagnostics are $1.88\times10^{-11}$,
$3.33\times10^{-16}$, and $1.35\times10^{-6}$, respectively.
Figure~\ref{fig:background} displays its radial profiles.

\subsection{Source factoring, conditioning, and independent shooting}

The three master equations are collocated on a Chebyshev--Lobatto interval.
Future-horizon regularity is built into the ingoing EF equations. In the UV we
write $F=e^{-p_FA}y$, with $p_F=4$, $2$, and $2\Delta-4$ for the quintuplet,
triplet, and rescaled singlet fields. The transformed operator is assembled
analytically, and the last tau row imposes $y'(r_{\rm cut})=0$. This removes the
non-normalizable coefficient at leading asymptotic order without exponentially
rescaling the discrete columns. The generalized pencil is passed directly to
a QZ driver; no inverse of $M_1$ is formed \cite{Jansen:2017oag}. With the numerical horizon
normalization used here,
\begin{equation}
 \w=2\omega_{\rm num},\qquad
 k_{\rm num}=\frac{\q}{2\sqrt{h_0^{\rm far}}}.
 \label{eq:rescaling}
\end{equation}

Resolution and domain errors are varied separately: $N=64,\ldots,160$ at
$r_{\rm cut}=10$, and $r_{\rm cut}=8,\ldots,12$ at $N=160$. Right-eigenfunction
overlaps track each branch, while left and right null vectors give
$\kappa_\omega=\sqrt{1+|\omega|^2}\|u\|\|v\|/
|u^\dagger M_1v|$. The largest condition estimate among accepted modes is
$2.1\times10^5$; the quoted digits are set by the observed envelopes, not by
the algebraic QZ residual.

An independent complex shooting solver starts at $r_0=10^{-5}$ with the
regular horizon relation, integrates with DOP853, and sets the fitted UV source
coefficient to zero. We vary $r_0$ from $5\times10^{-6}$ to $2\times10^{-5}$
and the extraction radius from 9 to 11. All accepted roots remain within
$3.0\times10^{-4}$ in $\omega_{\rm num}$ under these changes. Positive-real-
part poles are reported once; the reflected partner is implied.

They are excluded from every result below. This rejection illustrates why a
small residual of the discrete pencil is insufficient for a non-normal
spectral problem \cite{Jaramillo:2020tuu,Destounis:2021lum}.

\subsection{Controlled thermodynamic trajectories}

We continue the leading pole in each sector through 18 backgrounds on each of
the curves $\mu_B=0$ and $\mu_B/T=2$, spanning approximately
$87\leq T\leq346$ MeV. Every point is a shooting root, and the corresponding
factored-collocation eigenfunction is assigned by frequency and overlap with
its parent. The minimum parent overlap is $0.9985$; the maximum difference
between shooting and the $N=112$, $r_{\rm cut}=8$ spectral root is $0.012$ in
$\mathfrak w$. The complete table also records $(s/T^3,n_B/T^3)$, horizon
coordinates, branch and stability labels, distance to the CEP, and the parent
index.

Figure~\ref{fig:physical-trajectories} shows a common qualitative pattern on
the two paths. The quintuplet and singlet damping rates are nonmonotonic but
remain of order unity or a few tenths. The leading triplet changes much more
strongly: as the low-temperature endpoint is approached it moves to larger
real frequency while $-\operatorname{Im}\mathfrak w$ falls to
$3.7\times10^{-3}$. A dedicated endpoint study with $N$ up to 160 reproduces
this damping rate to $2\times10^{-7}$ in $\mathfrak w$, while variations of
the shooting start and UV extraction leave the root unchanged within the
reported tolerance. This gapped MAP-model triplet is long lived but
nonhydrodynamic.

\subsection{Comparison with the 2018 EMD realization}
\label{sec:old-emd-comparison}

A direct comparison with ref.~\cite{Rougemont:2018taa} must distinguish a
solver benchmark from model dependence.  We therefore replaced the PHA
potential, Maxwell coupling and energy scale by the functions and parameters
published in that reference, without changing the present collocation or
shooting algorithms.  A local reconstruction gives the earlier endpoint at
$(89.122,723.551)$ MeV, consistent with the rounded $(89,724)$ MeV quoted
there; the scaled local-cusp residual is $3.1\times10^{-4}$. At this endpoint
and at the two common neutral states used in that paper, all nine published relaxation times are reproduced within
$0.0111\,\mathrm{fm}/c$.  The largest shooting source residual is
$7.5\times10^{-9}$ and the largest spectral--shooting distance is $0.0074$ in
$\mathfrak w$.

Table~\ref{tab:old-emd-comparison} then compares the two models.  At
$T=400$ MeV their times differ by at most $0.004\,\mathrm{fm}/c$, displaying
the common ultraviolet behavior.  At $T=145$ MeV the PHA singlet relaxes about
ten percent faster, while the tensor and triplet remain close.  At their
respective endpoints both models have the ordering
$\tau_3>\tau_1>\tau_5$, but the PHA triplet time is about 37 percent larger.
Because the endpoint coordinates and calibrated potentials differ, this is a
cross-model comparison of homogeneous response, not a critical exponent or a
universality statement.

\section{Thermodynamic consistency of the finite-density benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark-consistency}

We tested the critical point in two independent ways.  First, we reconstructed
the algorithm distributed with the 2023 model release: second-order horizon
data, a relaxation field for the UV coefficient $\phi_A$, and crossings of
neighboring constant-$\phi_0$ trajectories.  Increasing the number of
trajectories from 100 to 800 moves the crossing estimate from
$(103.666,594.761)$ MeV to $(103.761,593.384)$ MeV.  Second, we solved the local
cusp equations.  With
\begin{equation}
 J=\frac{\partial(\log T,\log\mu_B)}
 {\partial(\phi_0,\Phi_1/\Phi_1^{\rm max})},
 \label{eq:thermo-jacobian}
\end{equation}
the fold condition is $\det J=0$ and the cusp additionally requires the
directional derivative of $\det J$ along the null vector of $J$ to vanish.
This gives
\begin{equation}
 (T_c,\mu_B^c)=(103.7553,593.3227)\ {\rm MeV},\qquad
 (\phi_0,\Phi_1/\Phi_1^{\rm max})=(3.528281,0.346374).
 \label{eq:independent-cep}
\end{equation}
The cusp is within $0.006$ MeV in temperature and $0.062$ MeV in chemical
potential of the 800-trajectory crossing estimate.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

For the maximum-likelihood PHA realization, source-factored collocation and
independent shooting validate the leading homogeneous quintuplet and singlet
poles and two triplet poles at the finite-density critical background. The
least-damped values are
$\w_5=1.8732(3)-0.8669(6)i$,
$\w_3=1.688542(4)-0.103168(6)i$, and
$\w_1=1.4924(12)-0.2561(13)i$. Three apparent roots of the original discrete
pencil are rejected by the independent source condition.

The same implementation reproduces the nine relaxation times quoted for the
earlier EMD model in ref.~\cite{Rougemont:2018taa}.  This cross-model benchmark
recovers their common high-temperature limit and shows a quantitative change
in the infrared: at the respective endpoints the PHA triplet relaxation time
is about 37 percent longer, whereas both models retain the ordering
$\tau_3>\tau_1>\tau_5$.

Along $\mu_B=0$ and $\mu_B/T=2$, shooting and overlap tracking give the same
qualitative ordering. The most pronounced feature is the gapped triplet pole:
its dimensionless damping falls by more than two orders of magnitude and
reaches $3.7\times10^{-3}$ at the low-temperature endpoint. The pole remains
oscillatory, and the endpoint value is stable under both spectral refinement
and shooting-domain variations. The quintuplet and singlet show milder,
nonmonotonic damping minima.

The equilibrium calculation now independently fixes the state.  It resolves
the two spinodals and equal-pressure coexistence line, and two different cusp
algorithms agree.  Their result differs from the HDF5 critical metadata by
$9.15$ MeV in chemical potential; the discrepancy is recorded without changing
the exact MAP parameters.  Conserved-charge evolution controls Maxwell drift,
while the neutral entropy and susceptibility are stable under the tested
finite-difference steps.

The scope is intentionally homogeneous. Coupled finite-momentum helicity-one
and helicity-zero calculations are required to establish shear, sound, baryon
diffusion, critical slowing, or spinodal growth, and none is inferred here.
Likewise, posterior uncertainty must be propagated before these MAP results can
be described as Bayesian predictions for real-time observables.

The interpretation retains the limitations of large $N_c$, strong coupling,
translational invariance, equilibrium backgrounds, and the bottom-up PHA
construction. The calculation concerns QNMs of a lattice-calibrated
holographic EMD model, not QNMs of QCD.

\section{Neutral thermodynamics and baryon susceptibility}
\label{app:thermodynamics}

As a numerical cross-check independent of the QNM pencil, we reproduce the
neutral entropy density and the second-order baryon susceptibility
\begin{equation}
 \chi_2^B(T)=\left.\frac{\partial(\rho_B/T^3)}
 {\partial(\mu_B/T)}\right|_{\mu_B=0}.
 \label{eq:chi2}
\end{equation}
For each of the 30 neutral backgrounds, the derivative is evaluated with the
centered ratio
\begin{equation}
 \chi_2^B(\delta)=
 \frac{(\rho_B/T^3)_{+\delta}-(\rho_B/T^3)_{-\delta}}
 { (\mu_B/T)_{+\delta}-(\mu_B/T)_{-\delta}},
 \qquad
 \delta=\frac{\Phi_1}{\Phi_1^{\rm max}},
 \label{eq:chi2fd}
\end{equation}
at $\delta=0.005$, $0.010$, and $0.020$. Charge-conjugation symmetry makes
$T$ and $s$ even and $\mu_B$ and $\rho_B$ odd in $\delta$; consequently, fixing
$\phi_0$ in this centered derivative differs from fixing $T$ only at
$\mathcal O(\delta^2)$.

Figure~\ref{fig:thermodynamic-validation} reports all 90 derivative evaluations.
On the finest step, the scan covers $74.69\leq T/\mathrm{MeV}\leq1003.70$,
$0.418\leq s/T^3\leq17.033$, and
$1.66\times10^{-6}\leq\chi_2^B\leq0.31869$. The largest displacement relative
to the $\delta=0.005$ result is $2.06\times10^{-3}$, attained by the coarsest
step. Across the same runs, the normalized Einstein-constraint residual remains
below $3.50\times10^{-9}$ and Gauss-law drift below $4.45\times10^{-16}$.
The complete values and diagnostics are stored in
\texttt{results/python/thermodynamic\_validation.csv}; no smoothing or fitted
interpolation enters the displayed points.

\section{Regular horizon expansion}
\label{app:horizon}

Writing
\begin{align}
 A&=A_1r+A_2r^2+\cdots,&h&=r+h_2r^2+\cdots,\nonumber\\
 \phi&=\phi_0+\phi_1r+\phi_2r^2+\cdots,&
 \Phi&=\Phi_1r+\Phi_2r^2+\cdots,
\end{align}
the compiled recurrence reproduces
\begin{align}
 A_1&=-\frac{2V_0+f_0\Phi_1^2}{6},&
 \phi_1&=V_{\phi,0}-\frac12f_{\phi,0}\Phi_1^2,\nonumber\\
 A_2&=-\frac{\phi_1^2}{12},&
 h_2&=\frac12(f_0\Phi_1^2-4A_1),\nonumber\\
 \Phi_2&=-\frac{\Phi_1}{2}\left(2A_1+\frac{f_{\phi,0}}{f_0}\phi_1\right).
\end{align}
Higher coefficients through fourth order are generated by truncated-series
arithmetic applied to the regular equations, rather than hand-entered formulas.

\section{Validation and claim scope}
\label{app:gates}

The authoritative machine-readable tables store background horizon data,
physical thermodynamics, branch and stability labels, frequency, resolution,
pencil and source residuals, condition number, tracking parent, and
eigenfunction overlap. A mode enters a figure or table only when continuum
convergence and independent shooting both pass. Generated finite-momentum
equations and their symbolic identity tests are supplied as reproducibility
artifacts, but no coupled finite-momentum spectrum enters the scientific claims
of this paper.
