\abstract{%
We determine the low-lying real-time spectrum of a Bayesian-calibrated
Einstein--Maxwell--dilaton model across its finite-density phase structure.
Solving the coupled helicity-one and helicity-zero fluctuations, we identify
the shear, sound, and baryon/heat-diffusion poles and reproduce independently
computed charged-hydrodynamic coefficients at small momentum.  At the
maximum-likelihood critical endpoint, diffusion vanishes as the susceptibility
diverges.  Fits along a constant-chemical-potential approach give the dynamic
exponent $z=4.08\pm0.05$, where the uncertainty is dominated by the scaling
window; a direct critical QNM fit gives
$-\operatorname{Im}\w\propto\q^{3.99}$.  Inside the spinodal branch at
$\mub=650$ MeV, the longitudinal pole develops a closed unstable band.  At
its midpoint we find $\q_*=0.283$, maximum growth
$\Gamma_*/(2\pi T)=1.41\times10^{-3}$, and band edge $\q_{\rm edge}=0.417$;
the band shrinks toward either fold.  A weighted 25-medoid compression of all
successful posterior draws represents the full posterior weight and yields
$z=4.075^{+0.010}_{-0.013}$ at 95\% credibility before the common fit-window
systematic.  All medoids retain negative diffusion inside the spinodal region.
These results provide a validated real-time characterization of critical
slowing and spinodal growth in a lattice-constrained holographic plasma.}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The real-time response of strongly interacting matter at nonzero baryon
density is difficult to determine from first principles.  Lattice QCD
constrains equilibrium thermodynamics near vanishing chemical potential, but
the sign problem obstructs direct access to the dense phase diagram and
Minkowski observables.  Bottom-up Einstein--Maxwell--dilaton (EMD) models offer
a complementary laboratory in which a lattice-constrained equation of state,
transport, and retarded response follow from the same black-brane solution.

A critical endpoint is a dynamical as well as a thermodynamic object.
Conserved densities, their mode mixing, and the divergence of static
susceptibilities determine critical slowing down.  The QCD critical endpoint
is expected to exhibit Model-H dynamics \cite{Hohenberg:1977ym,Son:2004iv},
whereas classical large-$N_c$ holography suppresses some nonlinear mode
couplings and can realize a different effective exponent
\cite{DeWolfe:2011ts}.  The exponent and the pole responsible for it must
therefore be extracted from the real-time spectrum rather than inferred from
the static equation of state.

The metastable branches of a first-order transition pose a second dynamical
question.  Between the two spinodal boundaries a thermodynamic curvature
changes sign, and a longitudinal hydrodynamic mode becomes unstable.  Full
gravitational dynamics must determine whether the instability occupies a
finite momentum band, its fastest-growing wavelength, and the range over
which a gradient expansion remains quantitative.  Holographic spinodal modes
have been studied in Einstein--scalar models
\cite{Janik:2015waa,Janik:2016btb,Caddeo:2024qgm}; the corresponding charged,
lattice-calibrated EMD calculation requires the complete helicity-zero system.

The PHA analysis of ref.~\cite{Hippert:2023bel} calibrated polynomial--
hyperbolic EMD models to lattice data and released the posterior ensemble
\cite{HippertZenodo}.  Homogeneous nonhydrodynamic QNMs were previously
studied in a different QCD-like EMD model \cite{Rougemont:2018taa}, and recent
work propagates the PHA posterior to equilibrium and horizon transport
\cite{Khan:2026uq}.  These results do not determine the coupled finite-
momentum pole structure, the critical diffusion law, or the full spinodal
band.  Charged-fluid studies further show that mode mixing and pole collisions
can sharply limit hydrodynamics \cite{Abbasi:2020ykq,Jansen:2020hfd}.

This work closes that gap.  We first reconstruct the horizon-data surface,
continue fixed-$\mub$ branches through both folds, and independently locate
the cusp of the thermodynamic map.  We then solve the coupled helicity-one and
helicity-zero perturbations in ingoing Eddington--Finkelstein coordinates.
Gauge-invariant sources are evaluated on a physical horizon basis after
quotienting exact residual-gauge solutions.  Adaptive DOP853 shooting and an
independent multidomain Chebyshev integration provide two radial methods.

On stable backgrounds, the resulting shear, sound, and baryon/heat-diffusion
poles reproduce a separately constructed charged-hydrodynamic evolution
matrix.  Along a fixed-$\mub=\mub^c$ approach, diffusion vanishes and the
susceptibility diverges; the inferred exponent is tested directly through
the critical QNM dispersion.  Inside the spinodal region, the full
helicity-zero pole is followed beyond the range where hydrodynamics predicts
unbounded $q^2$ growth, revealing a finite unstable band.

Finally, we propagate uncertainty through 25 weighted posterior medoids.  The
medoids are selected in joint parameter/critical-point space, include the
maximum-likelihood draw, and represent all 1589 posterior samples whose
released critical-point calculation succeeded.  For every medoid we
independently reconstruct the cusp, approach it along a physical path, and
locate both spinodals at $\mub^c+50$ MeV.  We keep numerical, scaling-window,
and posterior uncertainties separate.

Section~\ref{sec:model} defines the calibrated model and phase structure.
Sections~\ref{sec:perturbations} and \ref{sec:numerics} give the coupled
systems and validation.  Stable hydrodynamics, critical dynamics, spinodal
growth, and posterior propagation are presented in
sections~\ref{sec:stable}--\ref{sec:posterior}.  Appendices collect symbolic
identities, the error budget, homogeneous gapped modes, and reproducibility
details.

\section{PHA EMD model and phase structure}
\label{sec:model}

We use natural units, mostly-plus signature, and set the asymptotic AdS radius
to one.  The action is
\begin{equation}
 S=\frac{1}{2\kappa_5^2}\int \dd^5x\sqrt{-g}\left[
 R-\frac12(\partial\phi)^2-V(\phi)-\frac14 f(\phi)F_{MN}F^{MN}\right].
 \label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
The polynomial--hyperbolic parametrization is
\begin{align}
 V(\phi)&=-12\cosh(\gamma\phi)+b_2\phi^2+b_4\phi^4+b_6\phi^6,\nonumber\\
 f(\phi)&=\frac{\operatorname{sech}g(\phi)
 +d_1\operatorname{sech}(d_2\phi)}{1+d_1},
 &g(\phi)&=c_1\phi+c_2\phi^2+c_3\phi^3.
 \label{eq:pha}
\end{align}
The maximum-likelihood realization is sample 74 of the public posterior.  Its
exact binary64 parameters are stored in the machine-readable configuration;
the rounded values most relevant here are
\begin{align}
 \Lambda&=1123.870\ {\rm MeV},& \kappa_5^2&=11.37047,&
 \gamma&=0.590424,\nonumber\\
 (b_2,b_4,b_6)&=(0.327227,-0.0480134,0.000938515).&&&
\end{align}
The Maxwell parameters are
$(c_1,c_2,c_3)=(0.0088005,0.155799,0.0528185)$ and
$(d_1,d_2)=(1.71775,1679.18)$.  The large $d_2$ creates a narrow UV structure
in $f(\phi)$ that is retained in all backgrounds and perturbation kernels.

In domain-wall gauge,
\begin{equation}
 \dd s^2=e^{2A(r)}[-h(r)\dd t^2+\dd\bm{x}^2]+\frac{\dd r^2}{h(r)},
 \qquad A_M\dd x^M=\Phi(r)\dd t .
 \label{eq:background}
\end{equation}
We choose $r_H=A_H=h_H=\Phi_H=0$ and $h'_H=1$.  The independent horizon data
are $(\phi_0,\Phi_1)$, with
$|\Phi_1|<[-2V(\phi_0)/f(\phi_0)]^{1/2}$.  The conserved flux
$\mathcal Q=e^{2A}f\Phi'$ is evolved analytically, and the UV coefficients give
\begin{align}
 T&=\frac{\Lambda}{4\pi\phi_A^{1/\nu}\sqrt{h_0}},&
 \mub&=\frac{\Phi_0\Lambda}{\phi_A^{1/\nu}\sqrt{h_0}},\nonumber\\
 s&=\frac{2\pi\Lambda^3}{\kappa_5^2\phi_A^{3/\nu}},&
 \rhob&=-\frac{\Phi_2\Lambda^3}
 {\kappa_5^2\phi_A^{3/\nu}\sqrt{h_0}},
 \label{eq:thermodynamics}
\end{align}
where $\nu=4-\Delta$ and $\Delta=2+\sqrt{4+m^2}$.

\subsection{Cusp, coexistence, and spinodals}

Continuation is performed in horizon data rather than by isolated inversion
in $(T,\mub)$.  Folds satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \det\frac{\partial(\log T,\log\mub)}
 {\partial(\phi_0,\Phi_1/\Phi_1^{\rm max})}=0.
 \label{eq:fold}
\end{equation}
At the cusp, the derivative of this determinant along the null direction of
the same Jacobian also vanishes.  Along fixed chemical potential, pressure
differences follow from $\dd P=s\,\dd T$; equal pressure selects coexistence.

The $141\times121$ MAP horizon surface resolves 65 fixed-$\mub$ lines from
594 to 850 MeV.  Grid coarsening changes spinodal temperatures by at most
0.018 MeV and coexistence temperatures by at most 0.0085 MeV.  The independent
cusp is
\begin{equation}
 (T_c,\mub^c)=(103.7553,593.3227)\ {\rm MeV},\qquad
 (\phi_0,\Phi_1/\Phi_1^{\rm max})=(3.528281,0.346374).
 \label{eq:cusp}
\end{equation}
An independent 800-line crossing construction gives $(103.7611,593.3845)$
MeV.  The HDF5 metadata for sample 74 instead store
$(103.8978,602.4749)$ MeV.  Exact parameters and the reference UV prescription
were used, so the 9.152 MeV chemical-potential offset is retained as a
provenance discrepancy; the local cusp in eq.~\eqref{eq:cusp} defines the
dynamical background.

\section{Coupled perturbations and charged hydrodynamics}
\label{sec:perturbations}

We transform eq.~\eqref{eq:background} to ingoing Eddington--Finkelstein
coordinates,
\begin{equation}
 \dd s^2=-e^{2A}h\,\dd v^2+2e^A\dd v\dd r+e^{2A}\dd\bm{x}^2,
 \qquad A=\Phi(r)\dd v,
\end{equation}
and use $e^{-\ii\omega v+\ii kz}$.  Stable poles have
$\operatorname{Im}\omega<0$.  We report
$\w=\omega/(2\pi T)$ and $\q=k/(2\pi T)$; the horizon-normalized solver uses
$\w=2\omega_{\rm num}$ and $k_{\rm num}=\q/(2\sqrt{h_0})$.

At finite momentum the fluctuations split into $SO(2)$ helicities.  Helicity
two is the decoupled tensor field.  The helicity-one primitives are
$(H_{vx},H_{zx},a_x)$, while helicity zero contains
\begin{equation}
 (H_{vv},H_{vz},H_{zz},H_{aa},a_v,a_z,\varphi),\qquad
 H_{aa}=H_{xx}=H_{yy}.
\end{equation}
The complete linearized Einstein, Maxwell, and scalar equations are generated
from eq.~\eqref{eq:action}.  They remain linear in
$\sigma=-\ii\omega$ and use $p=\ii k$.

\subsection{Gauge invariants and source matrix}

In helicity one the physical combinations are
\begin{equation}
 Z_{\rm sh}=pH_{vx}-\sigma H_{zx},\qquad Z_J=a_x.
 \label{eq:h1-invariants}
\end{equation}
For helicity zero we use
\begin{align}
 Z_H={}&p^2H_{vv}-2p\sigma H_{vz}+\sigma^2H_{zz}
 +\left[p^2\left(h+\frac{h'}{2A'}\right)-\sigma^2\right]H_{aa},\nonumber\\
 Z_A={}&p a_v-\sigma a_z-\frac{p\Phi'}{2A'}H_{aa},\qquad
 Z_\phi=\varphi-\frac{\phi'}{2A'}H_{aa}.
 \label{eq:h0-invariants}
\end{align}
These combinations annihilate the exact residual radial-gauge orbits.  At a
trial frequency, regular horizon data span seven and three solutions in
helicities zero and one, respectively.  Quotienting four and one analytic
gauge solutions leaves three and two physical solutions.  Their UV source
coefficients form square matrices $S_0(\omega,k)$ and $S_1(\omega,k)$; QNMs
satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \det S_s(\omega,k)=0,
 \label{eq:source-matrix}
\end{equation}
or, equivalently, a vanishing smallest singular value.  This is the standard
coupled-field source-matrix construction \cite{Kaminski:2009dh}.

Metric, Maxwell, and scalar source/vev powers are respectively $u^0/u^4$,
$u^0/u^2$, and $u^\nu/u^\Delta$ for $u=e^{-A}$.  We extract each source by a
UV value/derivative combination rather than fitting an exponentially small
difference.  Primitive radial Einstein and Maxwell equations not used for
evolution are evaluated as constraints on the physical source-null solution.

\subsection{Independent hydrodynamic prediction}
\label{sec:hydro-formulas}

Local derivatives of $(T,\mub,s,\rhob)$ with respect to the two horizon data
are transformed to derivatives in $(\epsilon,\rhob)$.  The Maxwell relation
$\left.\partial s/\partial\mub\right|_T=
\left.\partial\rhob/\partial T\right|_{\mub}$ provides an independent check.
We use $\eta=s/(4\pi)$ and the charged Eling--Oz horizon
formula \cite{Eling:2011ct}
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\zeta}{\eta}=\left(s\frac{\partial\phi_H}{\partial s}
 +\rhob\frac{\partial\phi_H}{\partial\rhob}\right)^2.
 \label{eq:eling-oz}
\end{equation}
The finite incoherent conductivity of the translationally invariant EMD
plasma is \cite{Zhou:2015qui}
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\sigma_Q}{T}=\frac{2\pi\sqrt{h_0}\,f(\phi_H)}{\kappa_5^2}
 \left(\frac{Ts}{\epsilon+P}\right)^2.
 \label{eq:sigmaQ}
\end{equation}
Rather than using $D_B=\sigma/\chi$ outside its neutral limit, we diagonalize
the full linearized charged-fluid evolution matrix for
$(\delta\epsilon,\delta\rhob,\delta u_z)$.  It gives
\begin{align}
 \w_{\rm sh}&=-\ii D_\eta\q^2+\order(\q^4),\nonumber\\
 \w_\pm&=\pm c_s\q-\ii\Gamma_s\q^2+\order(\q^3),\nonumber\\
 \w_D&=-\ii D_B\q^2+\order(\q^4),
 \label{eq:hydro-dispersions}
\end{align}
including charge/heat mixing.  All coefficients compared with QNMs below are
computed from equilibrium and horizon data before any dispersion fit.

\section{Numerical method and validation}
\label{sec:numerics}

Backgrounds are integrated from a second-order regular horizon series with
DOP853, relative tolerance $3\times10^{-12}$, absolute tolerance
$3\times10^{-14}$, and maximum step 0.025.  The conserved electric flux is
used directly, and UV coefficients are extracted on a fixed sampling grid.
Across the production phase surface, normalized background constraints stay
below $4.8\times10^{-9}$ and Gauss drift below $9.0\times10^{-8}$.

For coupled QNMs, the characteristic horizon equations and the radial Maxwell
constraint determine the regular first derivative.  Differentiating the six
characteristic helicity-zero equations once and adding the noncharacteristic
Maxwell equation fixes the second horizon coefficient.  Including this term
is essential: a first-order start excites a constraint-violating DAE solution,
whereas the second-order recurrence makes primitive constraints converge with
the horizon start.

The primary source-matrix solver integrates the physical horizon basis with
DOP853 from $r_0=10^{-6}$ to $r_{\rm UV}=5$.  The independent method converts
the same second-order system to first order and solves it on eight Chebyshev
subdomains separated at
\begin{equation}
 r=(10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1},0.5,1.5,3),
\end{equation}
using 24 intervals per domain.  It neither calls an ODE integrator nor forms
the global primitive QZ pencil.  Resolution, UV radius, horizon start, and the
subdomain partition are varied independently.

\section{Critical slowing at the endpoint}
\label{sec:critical}

We approach the MAP cusp on the hot stable branch at fixed
$\mub=\mub^c$.  The horizon coordinate is displaced by
$0.5\geq\phi_0^c-\phi_0\geq0.005$, and the electric datum is solved anew at
every point to keep $\mub$ fixed.  Define the dimensionless distance
\begin{equation}
 r=\left[\left(\frac{T-T_c}{T_c}\right)^2+
 \left(\frac{\mub-\mub^c}{\mub^c}\right)^2\right]^{1/2}.
 \label{eq:reduced-distance}
\end{equation}
Over the asymptotic window, $D_B\sim r^{0.64--0.66}$ and
$\chi_B/T^2\sim r^{-0.62--0.63}$.  With the classical holographic static
anomalous dimension $\eta_{\rm stat}=0$, dynamic scaling gives
\begin{equation}
 D_B\sim\xi^{2-z},\qquad \chi_B\sim\xi^2,\qquad
 z=2-2\frac{\dd\log D_B}{\dd\log\chi_B}.
 \label{eq:z-scaling}
\end{equation}
Varying the fit over the last four through eight points gives
\begin{equation}
 z=4.08\pm0.05\quad\text{(fit-window systematic)}.
 \label{eq:z-hydro}
\end{equation}
The local thermodynamic Jacobian condition number rises from $1.3\times10^2$
to $2.8\times10^6$ along this path, while the Maxwell-relation residual stays
below $3.7\times10^{-6}$.

At the cusp itself, the $q^2$ coefficient vanishes and the full QNM determines
the leading gradient power.  Seven momenta from $\q=0.04$ to 0.15 give
\begin{equation}
 -\operatorname{Im}\w_D=A\q^z,\qquad z=3.99\quad
 (0.04\leq\q\leq0.08),
 \label{eq:critical-q4}
\end{equation}
with local exponents decreasing from 3.996 to 3.919 as the upper end is moved
to $\q=0.15$.  We quote $z=3.99(2)$ for the direct dispersion, where the
parenthesis covers this controlled window drift.  The largest critical-QNM
constraint is $9.1\times10^{-7}$ and the source determinant is below
$1.7\times10^{-11}$.

The homogeneous modes do not close at the cusp.  The least-damped gapped
triplet has $\w=1.68854-0.10317\ii$, while the leading singlet and tensor
dampings are 0.2561 and 0.8669.  Critical slowing is therefore carried by the
conserved longitudinal mode, not by an accidental homogeneous gapped pole.
This distinction is consistent with earlier holographic studies of
nonhydrodynamic critical behavior \cite{Finazzo:2016psx,DeWolfe:2011ts}.

\section{Spinodal growth and hydrodynamic breakdown}
\label{sec:spinodal}

At $\mub=650$ MeV the MAP fixed-chemical-potential line has folds at
$\phi_0=3.18138$ and 3.88392.  We solve the longitudinal spectrum at fractional
positions 0.1, 0.5, and 0.9 between them.  In all cases the signed hydrodynamic
diffusion coefficient is negative, so
$\operatorname{Im}\w_D=-D_B\q^2>0$ at small momentum.

Table~\ref{tab:spinodal} demonstrates a closed unstable band rather than only
a thermodynamic sign change.  The central maximum occurs at
$\q_*=0.2826$ with $\Gamma_*=0.001414$ and the pole returns to the lower half
plane at $\q_{\rm edge}=0.4175$.  Shooting at $\q=0.28$ agrees with nominal

The $q^2$ hydrodynamic approximation correctly gives the initial slope but
cannot close the band.  Its 10\% range ends at $\q=0.05$--0.08, well below both
the fastest-growing scale and the edge.  The maximum growth, preferred
momentum, and band edge all decrease toward either fold.  Three positions are
enough to establish this trend but not to claim a spinodal-boundary exponent;
we therefore do not fit one.

\section{Posterior robustness}
\label{sec:posterior}

The HDF5 posterior contains 1600 draws, of which 1589 have successful released
critical-point metadata.  Because these are already posterior draws, their
base weights are equal.  We standardize the 11 model parameters together with
the stored critical coordinates, retain principal components carrying 95\% of
the variance, and select 25 deterministic cluster medoids.  Each medoid is
weighted by its Voronoi population, and sample 74 is forced into the ensemble.
The resulting weights sum to one and represent all 1589 successful draws.

For each medoid, the cusp is independently located from the fold and
null-direction conditions rather than copied from metadata.  Adaptive

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have computed the coupled finite-momentum real-time spectrum of the
Bayesian-calibrated PHA EMD model.  Gauge-invariant source matrices, exact
residual-gauge quotienting, second-order horizon recurrences, primitive
constraint monitoring, and two independent radial integrations turn the
helicity-one and helicity-zero systems into controlled continuum QNM
calculations.

On stable backgrounds, the shear, sound, and baryon/heat-diffusion poles match
transport and thermodynamic coefficients computed without QNM input.  This
agreement identifies the longitudinal modes and verifies the charged-fluid
mixing.  The maximum relative difference in the dedicated $\q=0.05$
benchmarks is $1.7\times10^{-3}$, and extrapolated coefficients agree more
closely.

At the independently located MAP critical endpoint, the diffusion coefficient
vanishes while the baryon susceptibility diverges.  The hydrodynamic scaling
relation gives $z=4.08\pm0.05$, and the full critical QNM independently obeys
$-\operatorname{Im}\w_D\sim\q^{3.99}$ at small momentum.  Homogeneous gapped
modes retain finite damping; the conserved diffusive pole is the critical
degree of freedom.

Inside the $\mub=650$ MeV spinodal branch, diffusion crosses into the upper
half frequency plane and forms a closed unstable band.  At the midpoint its
fastest-growing scale is $\q_*=0.283$, its maximum dimensionless growth is
$1.41\times10^{-3}$, and its edge is $\q=0.417$.  Hydrodynamics controls only
the initial $q^2$ segment and cannot predict closure.  The band shrinks toward
both spinodal boundaries.

A weighted 25-medoid posterior analysis represents all successful released
draws and reconstructs every local cusp and spinodal pair independently.  It
gives a weighted 95\% range $4.062<z<4.085$ before the dominant common
fit-window error, and every medoid retains negative diffusion inside the
spinodal.  This justifies a Bayesian robustness claim for critical slowing
and the existence of spinodal growth, while MAP-only band scales are labeled
as such.

The interpretation remains subject to classical large $N_c$, strong coupling,
translational invariance, equilibrium backgrounds, and the bottom-up model
Ansatz.  The results characterize a lattice-constrained holographic model of
QCD, not QCD itself.  Natural extensions include correlator residues,
hydrodynamic/nonhydrodynamic pole collisions at complex momentum, nonlinear
spinodal evolution, and full-QNM propagation beyond the thermodynamic medoid
ensemble.

\section{Linearized identities and degree counting}
\label{app:identities}

The generated perturbation artifact contains three evolution equations and
one primitive Einstein constraint in helicity one.  Helicity zero contains
seven evolution/characteristic equations and the unused primitive equations
$E_{rr}$, $E_{rv}$, $E_{rz}$, and $M_r$.  Direct substitution verifies the
linearized Bianchi and Maxwell identities on every background.

The residual helicity-one orbit is proportional to
$(H_{vx},H_{zx},a_x)=(-\sigma X,-pX,0)$, so three primitives minus one gauge
orbit give the two physical sources in eq.~\eqref{eq:h1-invariants}.  In
helicity zero the four residual functions obey
\begin{equation}
 T'=0,\qquad R'=-A'R-\sigma T,\qquad
 Z'=-p e^{-A}T,\qquad \lambda'=0.
\end{equation}
Seven regular primitives minus four gauge directions give the three sources
in eq.~\eqref{eq:h0-invariants}.  The validation suite also checks transverse
parity, charge conjugation, the neutral limit, the $k\to0$ triplet and singlet
reductions, and all UV source/vev powers.  All ten symbolic tests pass.

\section{Numerical error budget}
\label{app:error-budget}

The coupled validation table is stored row by row, including frequency,
source determinant and singular value, gauge defect, primitive constraints,
horizon-recurrence condition, local principal-matrix condition, collocation
residual, $r_0$, $r_{\rm UV}$, resolution, and optimizer status.  A root is
accepted by the source residual even when MINPACK reports stagnation, provided
that the independently evaluated determinant, continuum envelope, and radial
cross-check pass.  Optimizer status alone is never a physics criterion.

For the critical exponent, five nested windows containing the last four
through eight thermodynamic points give $4.044$, $4.057$, $4.079$, $4.107$,
and $4.142$.  Their half-range sets the quoted $\pm0.05$ systematic.  The
direct QNM fits over four through seven low-momentum points give exponents
3.987, 3.982, 3.976, and 3.968.  Posterior and fit-window uncertainties are
reported separately because they arise from different sources.
